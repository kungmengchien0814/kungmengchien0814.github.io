👤 About Me

I am Meng-Chien Kung, a senior in the Department of Intelligent Automation Engineering, National Chin-Yi University of Technology, graduating in June 2027. Since 2024 I have worked in Prof. Peng-Jen Chen's research group on the flow-field and structural design of anion-exchange-membrane (AEM) water electrolyzers, with technical and equipment support from ITRI's Green Energy and Environment Research Laboratories. Over two years I have published seven conference papers and presented every one of them myself, two in English at international conferences.

Outside the lab I have worked with CNC/CAM machining, industrial robot arms and semiconductor processes, written computer-vision and CNC connectivity programs, and turned my capstone project into an interactive website. In graduate school I want to move beyond my current single-phase liquid-flow models and study gas–liquid two-phase flow in electrolyzer channels.

🔬 Research

AEM Electrolyzer Flow-Field and Structural Optimization

2024 – present | Advisor: Prof. Peng-Jen Chen | Support: ITRI Green Energy and Environment Research Laboratories

I build 3D COMSOL flow-field models of AEM electrolyzers. The work started with single-parameter comparisons and has since added experimental validation, parameter optimization and multiphysics coupling.

  • 2024HEFC 2024: Compared how the nickel-mesh layer and inlet/outlet diffusers affect pressure drop and flow uniformity; Excellent Paper Award (General Paper category).
  • 2024ACMT 2024: Ran a mesh-independence study and compared simulated pressure drop with ITRI experimental data; mean error about 0.006 bar at 4 million elements.
  • 2024–25CSME 2024 / ISME 2025: Optimized inlet diffuser angle, inlet flow rate and nickel-mesh thickness with response surface methodology and Newton's gradient method; the performance index (mean velocity / pressure drop) came out about 20% higher than the current product parameters.
  • 2025HEFC 2025: Single-cell model coupling flow, conjugate heat transfer and OH⁻ ion transport; main-channel velocity uniformity about 93% at an inlet velocity of 0.1 m/s.
  • 2025ILASS-Asia 2025: Joint flow-field and thermal-management design comparing channel aspect ratio, inlet diffusion, local ribs and the nickel mesh; first author, English oral presentation.
  • 2026ACRA 2026: Adding an outlet diffuser lowered pressure drop by about 34% against the baseline design; first author, English oral presentation; an output of NSTC project 114-2637-E-167-015.

Electrolyzer Geometry Optimization Using Machine Learning and Genetic Algorithms2nd Place

Junior-year capstone | Team of 3, team lead | 2nd Place, System Integration Applications category, 2025–26 Department Capstone Competition

We built a simplified five-layer 3D COMSOL model by coupling laminar flow, H₂ species transport and current distribution in stages, trained an MLP surrogate model on 200 simulation samples, and used a genetic algorithm to search channel parameters within machinable limits. I handled modeling and simulation, experiments and measurements, the project website and the Windows executable, and helped develop the Python, machine-learning and genetic-algorithm code.
